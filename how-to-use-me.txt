===============================================================================
  STEAM GAME DOWNGRADE HELPER  -  HOW TO USE ME
===============================================================================

WHAT THIS IS FOR

  Steam updates games automatically and gives you no way to refuse an update or
  go back afterwards. That is fine until a patch breaks a mod you rely on.

  This tool puts an older version of a game back on your PC, and leaves your
  mods alone while it does it.

  It was built after Elden Ring patch 1.17 broke Seamless Co-op and The
  Convergence, but it works for any Steam game you own.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  HOW TO RUN IT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  DOUBLE-CLICK:   Downgrade.bat

  That is the whole thing. Do not run the .ps1 file directly - Windows blocks
  scripts that came from the internet, and the .bat exists to get around that
  safely, for that one run only.

  Then answer the questions. It tells you what it is about to do at every stage
  and asks before doing anything. You can quit at any prompt.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT YOU NEED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Windows (10 or 11)
  * Steam, signed in, with the game installed
  * The Steam account that OWNS the game
  * Free disk space. Older versions are downloaded in full - for a big game
    that can be 50 GB or more. The tool checks before starting and refuses
    rather than filling your drive.
  * Your Steam password, ONCE, typed into a Steam window (see below)

  You do NOT need Python, or any other download or install. Everything else is
  handled for you, including installing SteamCMD if you do not have it.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ABOUT YOUR PASSWORD  -  PLEASE READ, IT LOOKS BROKEN WHEN IT IS NOT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  This tool never asks for, stores, or sees your Steam password.

  It opens a separate SteamCMD window (Valve's own tool) and you type your
  password into that directly. It happens ONCE. After that SteamCMD remembers,
  and future runs need no password at all.

  THE IMPORTANT BIT:

     *** NOTHING APPEARS ON SCREEN WHILE YOU TYPE YOUR PASSWORD ***

  No dots. No stars. No moving cursor. The line stays completely blank, exactly
  as if the keyboard has stopped working. This is deliberate on Valve's part.

  Just type it and press Enter.

  You can also PASTE it with Ctrl+V (or right-click). That is invisible too, so
  DO NOT PASTE TWICE thinking the first one failed. Paste once, press Enter.

  It is your normal Steam password. There is no separate SteamCMD account.

  Next comes Steam Guard, in one of two forms:

     - A prompt appears on your PHONE in the Steam mobile app. There is nothing
       to type - just approve it there. The window will sit saying
       "Waiting for confirmation..." until you do.

     - Or it asks you to type a 5-character code (email or authenticator).

  When you see this, it worked:

     Logging in user 'yourname' to Steam Public...OK
     Waiting for client config...OK
     Waiting for user info...OK

     Steam>

  Type  quit  and press Enter to close that window. The tool carries on by
  itself.

  AFTERWARDS, YOUR STEAM CLIENT MAY GO OFFLINE. See the next section - this
  catches people out and looks much worse than it is.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  IF STEAM GOES OFFLINE AFTERWARDS - THIS IS NORMAL, HERE IS THE FIX
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Signing SteamCMD in can knock the Steam client off its connection.

  You may see:

     - "NO CONNECTION" in the Steam client
     - "Unable to Sync - Steam was unable to sync your saves with the Steam
       Cloud" when you launch a game
     - a game update that fails instantly with "No connection"

  None of that is damage. Your game files are fine. Steam has simply lost its
  session because the same account signed in from SteamCMD.

  THE FIX:

     1. Steam menu > Exit. A FULL quit, not just closing the window.
     2. Start Steam again.
     3. If it still shows NO CONNECTION, use Steam menu > Go Online.

  Do this BEFORE you play anything.

  IMPORTANT: if you get the cloud sync warning, click Cancel, NOT "Play anyway".
  Playing while sync is broken can overwrite good cloud saves with whatever
  happens to be on this PC.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THREE THINGS THAT WILL UNDO YOUR DOWNGRADE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Get these wrong and you are back where you started, so they are worth knowing.

  1. "VERIFY INTEGRITY OF GAME FILES"
     Never use this on a downgraded game unless you WANT the newest version
     back. It compares your files against the NEWEST patch, decides your older
     files are damaged, and re-downloads them. One click undoes everything.

     It catches the change even when the files are the same size as the new
     ones - this has been tested, not assumed.

     Which also makes it the RIGHT way to undo a downgrade on purpose. To get
     back to the current version: right-click the game in Steam > Properties >
     Installed Files > Verify integrity of game files. Steam re-downloads only
     the files this tool changed.

  2. LAUNCHING FROM STEAM
     Launch the game from its mod loader instead - the .bat or launcher .exe
     your mod came with. Pressing Play in Steam starts the game through
     anti-cheat rather than the mod loader, and once a newer patch exists it is
     also what triggers the update.

     For Elden Ring with The Convergence, that means:
         ConvergenceER\Start_Convergence.bat
     For Seamless Co-op on its own:
         ersc_launcher.exe

  3. LEAVING AUTOMATIC UPDATES ON
     In Steam: right-click the game > Properties > Updates, and set
     "Only update this game when I launch it".

     Steam has no "never update" setting. That is the strongest one available.
     The tool checks this and warns you if it is wrong.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SAVE FILES - WORTH A THOUGHT FIRST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  This tool does NOT touch save files, and deliberately does not try to back
  them up.

  Every game handles saves differently, and there is no rule a script can follow
  to find them. Guessing wrong about somebody's saves is not acceptable, and a
  partial backup is worse than none because it looks like protection.

  Not every game needs you to do anything. Plenty keep saves on a server, or in
  Steam Cloud, where changing the game version makes no difference at all.

  But if the game you are reverting keeps its saves on your own PC, look up
  where it puts them and copy them somewhere safe before you start. Searching
  for the game's name plus "save file location" will normally find it in
  seconds, and the game's wiki usually says.

  Changing the version does not usually harm saves. But an older build may
  refuse to read a save written by a newer one, so it is worth the two minutes.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  IF YOUR SAVE WILL NOT LOAD AFTER DOWNGRADING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  This is the most common thing people run into, and nothing is broken.

  Games usually upgrade a save IN PLACE the first time it is loaded on a newer
  build. So if you played at all after the update landed, your save is now in
  the newer format, and the older build will not read it. Putting the older
  game files back does not put the save back - the two are separate things.

  WHAT TO DO:

     1. Look for a backup folder next to your save. Many mods keep timestamped
        copies automatically, without being asked.

     2. Restore the newest backup from BEFORE the update. You lose whatever you
        did after that point, and nothing else.

  For Elden Ring, saves live in:

        %appdata%\EldenRing\<your steam id>\

  and The Convergence keeps its backups in that same folder, named by date and
  time, so the right one is easy to pick out.

  IMPORTANT: if the game or a mod tells you to "fix it using Steam", DO NOT.
  That means Verify integrity of game files, which re-downloads the newest
  patch and undoes your downgrade - see the section above.

  This tool cannot do any of this for you. It does not know where your saves
  are, and a script that guesses wrong about save files does real damage. This
  part is yours.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT IT ACTUALLY DOES TO YOUR PC
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Installs SteamCMD, if you do not already have it, where YOU choose.
  * Downloads the older version of the game from Valve's own servers, using
    your own account. Nothing unofficial is involved.
  * Shows you the exact list of files it will replace, and waits for you to
    agree, before writing anything.
  * Replaces ONLY those files. It never deletes anything, which is how your mod
    folders survive untouched.
  * Checks afterwards that every file matches the version you asked for.

  If anything fails, it stops and tells you. It will not leave a half-changed
  game behind: each file is written to a temporary name and swapped into place
  in one step, so an interruption cannot corrupt a file.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  DISK SPACE, AND WHY IT KEEPS THE DOWNLOAD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The downloaded version is deliberately KEPT after the job is done.

  It seems wasteful, but it means that when the game gets patched again - and it
  will - running this tool a second time takes about two minutes instead of
  another hour, because there is nothing left to download.

  It lives inside the SteamCMD folder, which is why the tool asks you where to
  put SteamCMD, and suggests the same drive as the game.

  You can delete it whenever you like. The only cost is doing the download again
  if you ever need that version back.

  If you already downloaded a version before running this tool, it will find it
  and use it instead of downloading again.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CHOOSING WHICH VERSION TO GO BACK TO
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The tool reads Steam's own logs to find which versions your PC has had
  installed, and shows you how many times you played each one.

  That is usually the easiest way to pick: the version you played a lot, just
  before the current one, is nearly always the one you want. You do not need to
  know patch numbers.

  Two limitations, stated plainly:

     - Only versions THIS PC actually installed will be listed. If you
       reinstalled Steam recently, there may be little or no history.

     - Valve eventually removes very old versions from their servers. The tool
       checks availability early, using the smallest download, so you find out
       in seconds rather than after a long wait.

  If the version you want is not listed, you can look it up yourself at
  steamdb.info and enter the details manually. The tool prints the exact
  addresses to visit when it gets to that point.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Nothing was changed unless the tool explicitly said it was replacing files.

  To get back to the current version at any time: in Steam, right-click the
  game > Properties > Installed Files > Verify integrity of game files. That
  restores the newest patch, re-downloading only the files that changed.

  And if Steam is showing NO CONNECTION, restart Steam first - see the section
  above. A verify cannot run without a connection, and Steam will report that
  it validated everything when in fact it did nothing.

  This tool never touches your saves. If you backed them up before starting,
  they are wherever you put them.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  IS THIS ALLOWED?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Yes. It downloads games you own, from Valve's official servers, through
  Valve's own official tool (SteamCMD), signed in with your own account. There
  is no piracy, no cracking, and no third-party file hosting involved. It only
  ever fetches a version of a game your account already owns.
